## terminus rubric proposed

The rubric and its three project-local qualities live in `terminus-canon/projects/sexton/`, not here. Uncommitted and unratified — the blocking column is a proposal awaiting Michael.

- **Two pre-existing violations will surface on the first review; neither is a regression.** `agent.New()` returns `*Agent` from a package that also exports `Container` with `NewContainer`, where `constructor-convention` wants `NewAgent` (a bare `New` is idiomatic only for a package's single primary type). `mattermost.NewAlerter()` returns `*MattermostAlerter`, whose name matches neither its constructor nor its package without stuttering. Both are advisory. They are recorded here so the first review's output is not mistaken for something the review itself broke.

- **`camelcase-file-naming` has a canon gap that will produce a false positive.** `api/v1/sexton_grpc.pb.go` is snake_case because protoc-gen-go names it, and the quality's discrimination exempts `*_test.go` and build-tag suffixes but says nothing about generated files. Do not rename it and do not hand-edit it. The fix is a canon change to that quality's discrimination, which is Michael's call in the terminus-canon repo, not something to work around here.

- **`no-yaml-tags-on-dd-bound-types` was deliberately authored project-local rather than promoted.** It applies to every df/dd project in the constellation and belongs in `golang-conventions/`, but promotion is the operator's decision and the playbook forbids an agent making it. It sits under `projects/sexton/` so it is at least enforced somewhere while that decision waits.

- **Not proposed on purpose:** a quality encoding "a failure that could be benign errors the repo rather than completing the sync" — the sharpest rule this codebase has, and exactly what the broken-remote defect violates. It was left unwritten because it would harden the unratified posture inference (above) into an enforced review rule. Write it after the posture is confirmed, not before.

## terminus rounds 1-3

Three full-repo reviews ran against the new rubric. Terminus v1 records no dispositions, so this is the only record of what was decided.

- **A merge conflict was being committed and pushed, and the guard I documented did not cover it.** Round 3 found it; reproduced in a scratch repo. `validateBranch` catches an interrupted *rebase* because git detaches HEAD, but a merge or cherry-pick conflict leaves the repo on its branch — so `git add -A` swept up `UU` files and committed the raw markers. On a fleet that publishes them to every other machine. Fixed by `git.Unmerged` (`diff --name-only --diff-filter=U`) checked before staging and before the `pre_commit` hook. The claim that `validateBranch` alone made this impossible appeared in `AGENTS.md` and in the broken-remote card; both are corrected.

- **Vetoed, and it will come back.** Round 3 flagged capitalized comments in Go samples inside `docs/future/semantic-differs.md` under `lowercase-output`, whose territory is `**/*.go`. Territory narrows which qualities get selected, not where the reviewer may file, so a Go quality reached into markdown. Vetoed on that plus the content being illustrative code in a document scheduled for deletion. No canon change was made, so a future full review will raise it again.

- **An amendment is durable where a veto is not.** Round 2 would have re-raised `NewAmbiguousRepoError` returning `error`; it did not, because the exemption went into `constructor-convention`'s discrimination instead of into a conversation. When a finding is wrong about a *class* of code rather than one instance, amend the canon.

- **Do not read two clean rounds as convergence.** Round 1 found 4 advisory, round 2 found 2 different advisory, round 3 found the two blocking defects of the whole cycle. Each round is a cold read with no memory and samples differently — round 2 flagged 2 of 6 identical comment sites. Findings are samples, not inventories: when one lands, sweep for its siblings rather than fixing the cited line.

- **Deferred deliberately:** the no-remote classifier (still carded; it changes sync semantics on a live fleet and deserves its own cycle) and the porcelain parser's missing unmerged codes.

- **Unverified:** the five `docs/current/` documents were written from a full read of the packages they describe, but nothing was run against them. The error-classification claims in `git-and-commit-messages.md` in particular describe string matching against git's own output text, which is version-sensitive and was not exercised against a real conflict.
